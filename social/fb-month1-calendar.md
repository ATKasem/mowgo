## WEEK 2

### W2-Mon | Business Tip | Hormozi §13 (pricing) + §28 (8 value levers) | Psych: ANCHORING (Tversky & Kahneman 1974)
**Hook:** "Price your work by the hour and you will always be the cheapest guy in town."

The hour price makes you compare yourself to the kid with a $99 mower. The job price makes the client compare you to the guy who left ruts in their lawn.

One lawn, one price. One clean number they can say yes to. You are not selling 45 minutes of engine noise. You are selling a lawn that looks like the neighbor's and a check that clears.

Raise the anchor. The price is the first thing they remember about you.

**CTA:** "What do you charge per lawn right now? Do the math out loud in the comments."

### W2-Tue | Efficiency | Hormozi §44 (remove friction) | Psych: LOSS AVERSION + CURIOSITY GAP (Kahneman & Tversky 1979; Loewenstein 1994)
**Hook:** "You lost 2 clients last month. You do not know why."

They did not tell you. They just stopped answering. One of them told their neighbor, and the neighbor told two more.

The scary part is not the 2 you lost. It is the 6 you are about to lose and have no idea.

Ask every client one question when the season ends: "What would make next year better?" The answer is free. Ignoring it is not.

**CTA:** "When did you last ask a client why they stay? Honest answers only."

### W2-Wed | Relatable | Hormozi §9 (pain in their language) | Psych: IN-GROUP IDENTITY (Tajfel) + NEGATIVITY BIAS (Baumeister et al. 2001)
**Hook:** "The client who knows a guy who can do it cheaper."

The guy who can do it cheaper. The guy who has a mower and a cousin. The guy who will text them back in March and disappear in July.

We all know him. He is why you get the awkward phone call in August asking if you can squeeze them in.

You are not the expensive guy. You are the guy who shows up. There is a difference, and the lawn knows it.

**CTA:** "What is the cheapest 'guy' story you have ever had to fix? Drop it below."

### W2-Thu | Business Tip | Hormozi §41 (the math is the proof) | Psych: LOSS AVERSION (Kahneman & Tversky 1979)
**Hook:** "The $25 lawn takes 45 minutes and 3 follow-up texts. The $60 lawn takes 20."

Do the real math. Small lawn, big drive time, client who texts back a week later. That lawn is not $25. That lawn is $25 minus gas, minus 30 minutes of unpaid texting, minus the headache.

The bigger lawn is one pass, one invoice, one thank you. Same afternoon. Twice the money.

Your cheapest client is not the one who pays the least. It is the one who costs the most to keep.

**CTA:** "Name the lawn you would fire if you could. We all have one."

### W2-Fri | Efficiency | Hormozi §44 (leverage, remove friction) | Psych: IMPLEMENTATION INTENTIONS (Gollwitzer 1999)
**Hook:** "End-of-month invoice week is why you hate Fridays."

Monday: cut. Tuesday: cut. Wednesday: cut. Thursday: cut. Friday: sit in the truck and type invoices for two hours while the money waits.

Here is the fix. Finish the job, send the invoice from the curb before you start the truck. Ten seconds of work replaces two hours of Friday.

Pick one rule for next week: invoice same-day, every day, no exceptions. Write it down. That written rule is what makes it stick.

**CTA:** "Same-day invoicing or end-of-week? Why? Be honest."

### W2-Sat | Seasonal | Hormozi §26 (timeliness) | Psych: ATTRIBUTION / BLAME FRAMING (Heider 1958)
**Hook:** "The brown patch is not your fault. It is their sprinkler."

Clients water at noon, drown the roots, and blame the crew in August. You did not kill that lawn. The hose did.

One polite line when you see it: "That corner is drying out. Early morning watering would bring it back." You look like the expert. They feel like the student.

August is when you earn the September phone call. Give advice, not excuses.

**CTA:** "How many clients water their lawn at noon? Count them."

## WEEK 3

### W3-Mon | Business Tip | Hormozi §32 (pricing is everything) + §13 (close rates) | Psych: COMMITMENT & CONSISTENCY (Cialdini 1984)
**Hook:** "Raise your prices or raise your standards. Most crews do neither."

If you close 8 of 10 quotes, you are underpriced. If you close 3 of 10, your price is not the problem, your pitch is.

The fix is not a discount. The fix is a number you can say without flinching and a reason why that number is true.

Say the price like it is a fact, not a question. Then stay quiet. The silence does the selling.

**CTA:** "What is your close rate? Guess first, count later."

### W3-Tue | Efficiency | Hormozi §44 (friction removal) | Psych: ZEIGARNIK EFFECT (Zeigarnik 1927)
**Hook:** "The 3 texts you send every single week."

"Are you still coming?" "Can you move me to Thursday?" "Did you get my invoice?"

Thirty texts a week. Fifteen minutes a day. A hundred and twenty hours a year doing receptionist work for free.

The jobs are not the problem. The back-and-forth is. Every unanswered question in your phone is a small unpaid job waiting to happen.

**CTA:** "How many texts did you send last week? Guess."

### W3-Wed | Relatable | Hormozi §9 (pain specificity) | Psych: IN-GROUP + NEGATIVITY BIAS
**Hook:** "'Can you also trim the bushes while you are here?'"

The bushes. The weeds. The one branch that is definitely not in the contract. It is always while you are here, never before you come.

Say yes to the small ones. It builds trust. Say no to the big ones with a number attached. "That is a separate job, here is what it runs."

Free work is a habit. Paid work is a business.

**CTA:** "What is the wildest 'while you are here' request you have gotten?"

### W3-Thu | Business Tip | Hormozi §13 (fire the bottom 10%) | Psych: LOSS AVERSION + IMPLEMENTATION INTENTIONS
**Hook:** "You have 3 clients you would pay to get rid of. This week, do it."

You know the three. The late payer. The renegotiator. The one who texts at 9pm about one blade of grass.

Each one costs you more than the invoice says. Firing them frees the slot for a client who pays on time and says thank you.

One email. One phone call. "We are restructuring our route and cannot fit you next season." That is it. Do it before Friday.

**CTA:** "What is the worst thing a client has done that you tolerated anyway?"

### W3-Fri | Efficiency | Hormozi §41 (math as proof) | Psych: SPECIFICITY EFFECT + GOAL GRADIENT
**Hook:** "You drive past the same house twice on the same day."

Route math: two trips to the same street is double the gas and half the profit. Group the lawns, cut the street once, and add a lawn to the gap instead of a second drive.

One pass per street. That single rule saves an hour a day and a tank of gas a month.

The closer you get to done, the faster you move. A clean route is a finish line you can see.

**CTA:** "How many times a week do you drive past your own house on the way to a job?"

### W3-Sat | Seasonal | Hormozi §26 (timeliness) | Psych: SCARCITY (Cialdini; Worchel et al. 1975)
**Hook:** "September is coming. That is your money month."

Fall cleanup bookings start in two weeks. The crews that book them now are the crews that get them.

One email to every client: "Fall cleanup slots open October 1. We take 20 a week. First come gets the date they want."

Real scarcity, not fake. When the slots fill, they fill.

**CTA:** "Have you told your clients about fall cleanup yet? Yes or no."

## WEEK 4

### W4-Mon | Business Tip | Hormozi §27 (speed-to-lead) | Psych: RECIPROCITY + FLUENCY (Cialdini; Regan 1971; Alter & Oppenheimer)
**Hook:** "Your prices are fine. Your response time is not."

A client texts three crews. The first one answers in 4 minutes and books the job. The other two answer tomorrow and wonder why business is slow.

Speed is free. It costs nothing to answer fast and it outbids every discount in town.

One rule: every lead gets an answer within the hour, even if the answer is "Thursday works, I will send the quote tonight."

**CTA:** "How fast did you answer the last quote request you got? Be honest."

### W4-Tue | Efficiency | Hormozi §44 | Psych: COGNITIVE EASE (Kahneman; Alter & Oppenheimer)
**Hook:** "You do not have a receptionist. You have a phone."

The phone is your office. The texts are your paperwork. The calendar in your head is a liability.

One list. One place where every job, every price, every follow-up lives. When the phone rings mid-job, the answer is already written down.

Simple beats clever. The crew with one list runs circles around the crew with twelve notebooks.

**CTA:** "Where do your jobs live right now? Head, notebook, or app?"

### W4-Wed | Relatable | Hormozi §9 | Psych: MERE EXPOSURE + IN-GROUP (Zajonc 1968)
**Hook:** "The gate code saga, part 47."

They text you the code at 8:15. You pulled up at 7:45. The code was wrong anyway.

Every crew has a gate code story. Every single one. That is the thing about this job, you are never alone in the dumb stuff.

We keep a notebook in the truck for a reason.

**CTA:** "Post your gate code story. Best one wins bragging rights."

### W4-Thu | Business Tip | Hormozi §21 (know the game) + §24 (ICP) | Psych: STATUS QUO BIAS (Samuelson & Zeckhauser 1988)
**Hook:** "You are paying for 20-person features you will never touch."

Big software prices itself for companies with office managers and HR. You are a crew of one to three. You do not need a call center module. You need the jobs, the invoices, and the rain.

Look at your last software bill. Count the features you actually touched this month. That is what you are really paying for.

**CTA:** "What do you actually use in your software? List it."

### W4-Fri | Efficiency | Hormozi §44 (friction) + §41 | Psych: DEFAULT BIAS + ENDOWMENT EFFECT (Thaler)
**Hook:** "The 'invoice sent' text that gets read and ignored."

You sent it. They saw it. They will pay it. Eventually. Maybe.

Make paying easier than ignoring: a link in the text, a tap, done. The client is not lazy. They are busy. Remove the busy.

Get paid faster by making the payment the path of least resistance.

**CTA:** "How long does the average client take to pay you? Real number."

### W4-Sat | Seasonal | Hormozi §26 | Psych: PEAK-END RULE
**Hook:** "The last mow of summer is the one they remember."

All season they barely notice you. Then September hits and the lawn looks better than the street. That is the mow they talk about at dinner.

End strong. Edge the walkways. Blow the clippings off the drive. The last impression is the one that books next year.

**CTA:** "What is the one thing you always do on the last mow of the season?"

## WEEK 5

### W5-Mon | Business Tip | Hormozi §49 (referrals) + §27 (proof) | Psych: RECIPROCITY + SOCIAL PROOF (Cialdini 1984)
**Hook:** "Word of mouth is not a strategy. It is a reward."

Word of mouth happens when you do the work. It is not something you can turn on. But you can point it in the right direction.

Ask for the referral at the moment of the win. Right after the transformation, when the lawn looks unreal, that is when they say yes. "Know anyone with a lawn like yours? Send them my way."

One ask per happy client. That is the whole system.

**CTA:** "When did you last ask a happy client for a referral?"

### W5-Tue | Efficiency | Hormozi §44 | Psych: LOSS AVERSION (time = money framing)
**Hook:** "You spend 4 hours a week doing receptionist work for free."

Confirming. Rescheduling. Chasing invoice payments. Answering "are you still coming" seven times a day.

Four hours a week is a full month of evenings a year. That time has a price. Every hour you spend texting is an hour you are not cutting, quoting, or resting.

The job is mowing. The texts are overhead. Cut the overhead.

**CTA:** "Rough guess: how many hours a week do you spend on texts and calls?"

### W5-Wed | Relatable | Hormozi §9 | Psych: NEGATIVITY BIAS + IN-GROUP
**Hook:** "Client texts at 9pm: 'Is the lawn okay?'"

Is the lawn okay. The lawn is fine. The lawn has been fine since 2019. The lawn is the healthiest thing on the block.

You reply anyway. Because that is the job. Reassurance with a side of grass.

**CTA:** "What is the most unnecessary question a client has ever asked you?"

### W5-Thu | Business Tip | Hormozi §9 (review ask mid-value) + §6.4 (proof over promise) | Psych: SOCIAL PROOF + COMMITMENT
**Hook:** "Your reviews are your sales team."

People pick a crew the way they pick a restaurant. They check the reviews. No reviews, no lawn.

Ask for the review right after a win. Not at signup. After the tenth mow, after the rain save, after the moment they said "this looks amazing."

One line in your reply: "If you are happy, a 5-star review helps a small crew a lot." That is the whole ask.

**CTA:** "How many reviews does your business have? Count right now."

### W5-Fri | Efficiency | Hormozi §41 | Psych: ZEIGARNIK + SPECIFICITY
**Hook:** "Double-booked yourself again?"

Two clients, same Tuesday, both expecting you at 9. You find out at 8:15.

The fix is not a better memory. It is one calendar that cannot be in two places at once. Every job gets a slot. Every slot gets one job. When the slot is full, the answer is no before it becomes a problem.

A schedule you can see is a schedule you can trust.

**CTA:** "When was the last time you double-booked? What happened?"

### W5-Sat | Seasonal | Hormozi §50 (seasonality = feature) | Psych: URGENCY (deadlines work)
**Hook:** "October lawns do not care about your calendar."

The leaves will fall whether you are ready or not. The crews that book October in September are the crews that get paid in October.

One message: "Leaf cleanup slots are going fast. Want yours?" Send it now.

**CTA:** "Is your October calendar booked or empty? Be honest."

## WEEK 6

### W6-Mon | Business Tip | Hormozi §13 + §32 (charge the outcome) | Psych: ANCHORING + VALUE FRAMING
**Hook:** "Charge what the result is worth, not what the hour is worth."

The client is not buying 40 minutes. They are buying a lawn that makes the neighbors ask who did it. That result is worth more than your hourly rate says.

Price the job. Deliver the result. Stop apologizing for the number and start owning the outcome.

The hour is your cost. The result is their value. Sell the second one.

**CTA:** "What is the highest-priced lawn you have ever quoted, and did you get it?"

### W6-Tue | Product (soft, max 1 in 20) | Hormozi §44 + §9 | Psych: LOSS AVERSION (client-side anxiety)
**Hook:** "The 'are you still coming' text is a client saying they are nervous."

Rain clouds roll in and every client wonders. They text you. You text them back. Seven times. That is not a client problem. That is a system problem.

One message to everyone at once: "Rain today, your job moves to Thursday, same time." One tap beats seven texts.

The crew that communicates once wins the nervous clients.

**CTA:** "What do your clients say when it rains? Share the best one."

### W6-Wed | Relatable | Hormozi §9 | Psych: ASPIRATION + SOCIAL IDENTITY
**Hook:** "You know you have made it when..."

...the client stops asking your name and starts asking your schedule. ...you can say no to a lawn and mean it. ...your invoice is sent before you leave the driveway.

Small wins. They count. Say one out loud.

**CTA:** "Finish the sentence: you know you have made it when..."

### W6-Thu | Business Tip | Hormozi §9 (sell at deprivation) | Psych: STATUS QUO BIAS + LOSS AVERSION
**Hook:** "The price of doing nothing."

Staying on paper costs you: missed jobs from double-booked days. Invoices that go out late and get paid later. Clients who think you forgot them.

None of it is dramatic. All of it is money. The quiet leaks are the ones that sink crews.

Doing nothing is a choice. It just does not look like one from the inside.

**CTA:** "What is the most expensive mistake you have made that was really just doing nothing?"

### W6-Fri | Efficiency | Hormozi §23 (money model: upsells) | Psych: ANCHORING (Tversky & Kahneman 1974)
**Hook:** "The $20 add-on is the $200 client."

Mulch. Weeding. The bushes that are definitely in the contract (they are not). Small jobs are small money only if you never offer them.

One line at the end of every job: "While I am here, the beds need an hour. Want it done?" Half say yes. That is $20 to $60 of pure margin on a lawn you were already at.

The upsell is not pushy. It is helpful. You are already there.

**CTA:** "What add-on do you always forget to offer?"

### W6-Sat | Seasonal | Hormozi §42 (the 3 numbers) + §50 (slow months = systems) | Psych: IMPLEMENTATION INTENTIONS (Gollwitzer 1999)
**Hook:** "The 3 numbers to fix before the mower goes away."

Before the season ends, run the numbers: your price per lawn, your close rate on quotes, your response time to leads.

One number up changes everything. Ten percent on price, without losing a client, is pure profit for next year.

Pick one number. Write it on a sticky note. That sticky note is next spring's plan.

**CTA:** "Pick one number to fix this winter. Put it in the comments so it is real."
