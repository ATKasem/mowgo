How Much Does Lawn Care Software Cost in 2026?

If you run a lawn care business, you've probably typed "lawn care software pricing" into Google and come out more confused than you went in. Every vendor says they're the affordable option. Most of them are quoting you a price that covers one person, one feature, and none of the stuff you actually need.

Here's the honest state of pricing in 2026, what the real numbers are, and where the traps are.

The real price range

Lawn care software runs from $0 to $700+ a month. Most of that range is wasted money for a small crew.

A typical 5 to 10 person lawn business should expect to pay $150 to $350 a month on a full-featured platform. A solo or two-person crew does not need to pay that.

The trap: per-user pricing

This is the one that quietly bleeds lawn care businesses dry, because lawn care scales by adding crew, and per-user software scales your bill right along with them.

Jobber's math is the clearest example. Core starts at $39 a month for one user. Add a helper and you jump to Team Connect at $169 a month for five users. That's not a $29 fee. That's a $130 jump the day you hire your first crew member.

Grow your crew to seven people and you're looking at $349 a month on Team Grow, plus $29 per seat beyond the cap. Then the add-ons start: AI Receptionist at $99, Marketing Suite at $79.

A lawn company with three trucks on Jobber can easily spend more than $400 a month before they've bought anything that optimizes a route.

MowGo $39 flat vs. Jobber $169 team plan

The same crew on Jobber pays $169 on the cheapest team plan, before add-ons.

That's the difference between software that grows with you and software that taxes every hire you make.

What actually matters more than price

Price is the number you compare. It is not the number that decides whether the software pays for itself. That's crew-hours.

A two-person crew losing 40 minutes a day to a bad route, at $35 an hour loaded labor cost per man, burns roughly $47 a day. That's about $565 a month. Route optimization that claws back even half of that pays for the software before you count a single new job.

The same 40 reclaimed minutes is about one extra $55 mow per day. That's $1,320 a month in found revenue on the same payroll. Software doesn't cut grass. It decides how many lawns the same two people can cut.

So the real question for any platform is not "what does it cost per month" but "does it put the next recurring visit on the calendar automatically, and does it keep Tuesday's stops in the same part of town?" Everything else is decoration.

The free tier, honestly

Yardbook's free tier is genuinely free and genuinely useful. If you're a brand-new solo with ten lawns and a borrowed trailer, that's where you should start. The ads and the payment surcharge are a fair trade at that stage.

The problem comes when you grow. Yardbook's paid plans are $34.99 per user per month on Business and $49.99 per user on Enterprise. A seven-person team pays $245 to $350 a month for software that was free six months earlier. Same per-user trap, just delayed.

MowGo's free tier is different in one respect: it's capped by clients, not by time. Five clients free, forever, no card, no trial clock. When you outgrow it, the paid plan is $39 flat, not $34.99 per person.

What you should actually do

  1. If you're a solo with a small book and you don't want to pay anything, use the free tier of something. Yardbook or MowGo. Learn the habits, build the book.
  2. If you're hiring your first crew member this season, do the per-user math before you sign anything. A $39 plan plus one $10 crew seat beats a $169 team plan that does the same thing.
  3. If you're running 300+ recurring accounts with office staff, Service Autopilot's automation engine is worth its price and its setup time. That's the one case where the heavy tool earns its keep.
  4. If you run chemical applications across multiple crews, RealGreen is the industry standard for a reason. If you mow, it's a freight train for a grocery run.

The software that wins is the one that survives the season. The one that charges you more every time you hire is not that.
