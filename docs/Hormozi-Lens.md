## When to use
Growth strategy, marketing/advertising decisions, churn/retention work, "what should I do with my time/money" questions, pricing-adjacent packaging, hiring/talent, or any founder-decision where a framework helps. Also when reviewing landing pages, ads, content, onboarding, or retention flows.

## 1. Three questions for making bets
- Proposed action → **"What problem are you trying to solve?"** (most proposals are emotional discomfort; lack of focus = intellectual laziness).
- You want something → **"What would it take?"** (assumes success; forces pricing the path, not vetoing it).
- Anything vague → **"What do you want to have happen?"** then chain: **what does that mean? / how do you know? / so what?** then "what else gets a higher return with this time?"
- **"Do nothing" is the undefeated champion** — keep allocating to what's already returning.

## 2. AI ≠ the answer
- AI is one leverage; it doesn't cancel capital, media, teams, one-to-many, async, sales education. Most AI use = doing MORE low-priority things faster.
- **Making good decisions is higher leverage than AI** ("deciding something is not a priority beats automating a non-priority").
- Test: **"Has AI made me more money? No → wrong place."** Find the TRUE constraint; focus the tiny speck of resources there. AI-first = data-first.

## 3. Retention playbook (School/Sam Ovens data)
- Track joins vs cancels monthly; label growing/flat/declining. Benchmarks: platform avg 20% churn; good <10%; bad >30% (fix retention first).
- **Churn drops with time:** months 1–3 = 20%+; past day 90 → ~10%; past month 6 → ~2% (5x LTV). Question is never "lower average churn" but **"get them past day 90 / month 6."** Month 1 = activation/expectations; day 90 = deliver an outcome; month 6 = connect with others.
- Tactics: ask leavers why (tag reasons, fix top 1-2 per 20 cancels); ask best members why they stay (that's your retention engine; fix by subtraction — "remove the suck around the good thing"); tier by evidence (ideal customers say price → add lower tier; non-ideal → price is a filter); **overwhelm is #1 non-price churn driver** ("delete everything, what would you fight for?" survey; value per second, one valuable action/week); design for busy + power users; front-load the sticky thing + fast win in 24h; show up daily and care; weekly live call + recording; annual plan (16-17% off, decide once); 10 regulars + matchmaking (friend = 5x retention).
- Growth = flat retention curve that compounds. One thing at a time.

## 7. Service-business scaling rules
- **Input-output equation** first; diagnose demand- vs supply-constrained before anything.
- Stuck local: order = pricing/packaging → cash flow → attribution → ads → content/thought-leadership (well). Local = high trust (1-call close), small market (dominate or expand radius via brand).
- **SMB customers: only two models work — super cheap + automated ($400/mo, nothing to deliver) or upmarket (they know their metrics). The middle is a dead zone.** CAC never cheaper than today; SMB stick at $1.5-3k = 4-6 months.
- Don't solve problems that don't exist (AI narrative ≠ metrics); 29-40 month stick = the game; prepay to offset CAC; AI cuts headcount → margin.
- **AI-first = data-first.** Sequence: fix supply constraint (offshoring 2-3x leverage) → data layer → AI layer → then market.
- Regrets = imagined upside without the suffered downside; either want less or trade more. **The who game:** talent levels ($50k→$1M employees); best talent is always in the future; keep passive passive; **punch card — 4-5 big seasons left**.
- Sales constraint → build a **supply-gen machine parallel to demand gen** (app-gen → nurture → interviewing → onboarding → retention/ascension of salespeople). Cost-to-acquire-talent vs lifetime GP/employee = 5:1+ returns.
- Real limiter: people don't believe in your vision enough to work for you. Business = find world-class people, get out of their way; win on character; punch above your weight; build the talent funnel, don't be mean.

## 9. Copywriting masterclass (sKOcCQh5nac — David Perell "How I Write" interview, 1M+ books sold)
- **Utility & validity** (framework filter): valid = true in many situations (MECE); useful = materially changes behavior/decisions. "How do I break this model? If I can't, it's done." Value equation is the gold standard — nobody has changed the 4 variables. Zero theory, only what you DO ("what does someone do as a result?" = #1 cut filter). Learning = same condition, new behavior; rate of learning = intelligence.
- **Pain is the pitch** — two weapons: promise AND pain (most overemphasize promise). **Sell at the point of greatest deprivation, not satisfaction** (steak story: full = no, starving = two steaks). Sell value only when it CREATES a new problem you can solve (leads solved → they need lead-working = "hungry for dessert"). **Pain happens in moments — specificity of pain = credibility** ("you can't know this pain this well and not be able to deliver"; Leila's coveralls/chafing/second-row-in-pictures). Describe the prospect's pain in their own language and persuasion follows from perceived knowledge.
- **#1 Creator Mistake: building new products for your audience instead of building MORE audience for your product.** Product sales reinforce the wrong activity (the audience made the money); end state = 6 businesses, attention split, all crap. Products need repurchase/consumable/recurring or you're condemned to new-product treadmill. Steady one-product growth beats pops.
- **Title/subhead A/B testing (real results):** "$100M leads" beat promotion/advertising/marketing (people want the OUTPUT, not the activity); "get STRANGERS to want to buy" beat "get MORE people" 71/29; "how to" prefix beat concision 70/30 (concision lost — clarity won). Optimize for the cold audience's one split-second decision, not your fans. Back cover = mini sales letter: chapters → benefit bullets (specificity = credibility: "six-part ad framework that gets MORE people, ESPECIALLY strangers, to want what you sell").
- **Writing process:** write only on 6h+ free days (Zeigarnik/open loops); TOC first (descriptive chapter titles, hardest part); chapters = story → description → plentiful examples → notes; words first, doodles later; ~10 drafts (19 for Leads, full rewrite at draft 12 after 10 readers); "coats of paint" editing — crunch words down until removal would hurt; many comments on one section = delete/rewrite it (Stephen King method); only ~10% of ideas make the cut; "if people saw how much work I put in, they wouldn't think it's as exceptional" — **master the middle** (marathon: champions made in the 26.2 miles, 95% of crowds at start/finish).
- **Books as assets:** timeless assets, not lead magnets — "the ultimate lead magnet is a timeless asset"; 99% never buy but create your reputation — give them exceptional value anyway. Evergreen test: backtest against 2,000 years ago (people always wanted fast/easy/risk-free; one-to-one and one-to-many always existed); $100M Leads mentions zero platforms. "How you launch a book = how good you are at marketing; how it sells in 2 years = how good the book is." Review ask mid-book (after value), not page 5.
- **10x work → 100-1000x word of mouth:** gold medal vs 4th place (small performance gap, enormous practical gap); returns come at the END (16th coat of paint); internet = global competition (curse) + global reach (gift); Naval: "technology democratizes consumption and consolidates production"; build assets, not magazines with hard covers (Rich Dad Poor Dad: 100k copies/mo decades later).
- **Deep vs wide:** wide = useful only for beginners; deep = useful for anyone; sweet spot = deep AND wide (first-five-clients advice is wide; strategy is deep). **Big Domino** (Tim Ferriss): one action that makes the rest of the to-do list irrelevant — for acquisition.com it's proprietary deal flow: more people wanting to deal with you than you can handle, then keep raising the quality bar.
- **Writing rules:** few words, simpler words, sentence rhythm (short-short-long), lower grade language for maximum comprehension ("concision only as a tool for comprehension"), "short words, short sentences, big promises"; practice = constrained writing (X/Twitter = fast feedback, Hemingway app, no adverbs).
- **Videos:** unscripted except intro; hooks lifted cross-industry (study high performers in OTHER industries, adapt); 80/20 effort on hook/packaging/title/thumbnail/first 30-60s; congruence between click and content.

## Landing-page review rubric (applied to MowGo Aug 2026)
Fast checklist when reviewing any landing/compare page for conversion:
1. **Hook** (proof, promise, plan) in H1 + subhead; outcome-led headline, not category-labeled.
2. **Buyer awareness:** unaware visitors need problem+category first; deep-cut competitor links ("vs Ruunly") waste nav slots — keep one "Compare" destination.
3. **Clear not clever:** grade-level copy, one idea per sentence, cut "better/great/powerful/solution".
4. **Proof over promise:** product screenshots > claims. Real screenshots from the live demo app beat AI mockups (which render gibberish text).
5. **Risk reversal at decision point:** guarantee line directly under final CTA, not buried.
6. **Fact-lock rule:** never add/change/remove prices, dates, stats, or feature claims during copy rewrites; verify claims against each other ACROSS pages (e.g. "route optimization live" on one page vs "coming soon" on another = HIGH). Jobber verified numbers (Aug 2026): Connect $139/mo month-to-month / $99 annual, +$29/mo per additional user.
7. **CTA path check:** every "Start Free"-labeled CTA must land on signup, not login (`/login?mode=signup`); preserve plan intent (sessionStorage → auto-checkout after auth).
8. **Exclusivity claims** ("only app", "0 competitors") almost always contradict your own comparison table — reword to "included on the free plan".
9. **Source rule (added 2026-08-04 after the 1,140+ incident):** every number on the page must trace to an intel/ file or a verified source (Census CBP files pull directly from census.gov, not search snippets). Round DOWN when converting census counts to "+" claims (1,022 → "1,020+"). Never ship an unsourced stat — one falsifiable number poisons the whole page's credibility (negative WOM is 5-37x stronger).

## 10. Affiliate launch machine (DaRXece2ItE — behind the $100M Leads launch: 500k registrants, ~⅓ from unpaid referrals)
- **Affiliate ≠ referral:** affiliates own businesses and advertise; they offer your stuff to their engaged leads for money, free stuff, or both. Treat the affiliate as a NEW customer — start with the offer TO them.
- **Two-tier, three-reward structure** (capture whales + long-tail): Top 10 = fireside 1:1 Q&A + dinner (big unscalable prize); Top 50 = 3-hour in-person Q&A at HQ; 10+ opt-ins = exclusive audiobook (70+ one-min tactics) + raffle for 50 seats + recording. Each tier approximates the one above.
- **Minimum activation threshold rule:** set the floor by friction — in-person visit = 2, text intro = 4-5, low-friction opt-in = 10+. "If friction is low, make the threshold higher."
- **Win-win-win:** prizes must benefit you, the affiliate, AND their audience — so the affiliate never feels like they're hard-closing their list (e.g., audience questions flow through the affiliate's link).
- **5-step machine:** 1) build the affiliate offer → 2) build the place (affiliate sales page: prizes, what to do, how) → 3) promote the program (email list max 2x, re-engage prior affiliates, CTA on post-purchase thank-you page, content) → 4) onboard with fast feedback (custom tracking link + real-time leaderboard) → 5) supply collateral (pre-written emails/posts/ads/texts; initial batch + "winners" batch once data lands).
- **BTS data sharing = retention:** share real-time launch performance (winning emails, ads) with affiliates — the most underrated component; turns affiliates into students of the launch.
- **Leaderboard cadence:** send updates daily; personally reach out to climbers and offer to help them promote more. Support EVERY affiliate type (video creators, writers, ad buyers, text-sharers) — "as soon as there's friction, they'll stop."
- Core four loops: use outreach/email/content/ads to RECRUIT affiliates, then the affiliates use them for you.
- Quotes: "If you make the offer good enough, you can move mountains." / "I'd rather help you out than give a king's ransom to the social media platforms." / "Don't lie. Don't exaggerate. Just state the facts, tell the truth."
- Novel: non-monetary comp as default (no 1099, can beat 10% kickbacks), BTS data as retention, prizes for the affiliate's AUDIENCE, raffle for the long tail, affiliate-only audiobook as a product-within-a-product.

## 51. The two numbers — LTV:CAC (Ep 985)
