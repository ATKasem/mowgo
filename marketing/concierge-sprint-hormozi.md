## The frame (say this to yourself, not the prospect)

"You are not selling software. You are selling **never losing another customer to a missed text** — and I'll set it up for you for free."

Hormozi's rule: sell the outcome, not the tool. "Scheduling software" is a feature; "your phone stops ringing at 9pm with 'can you come Thursday?'" is an outcome.

## The 5-step sprint sequence (per lead)

### Step 1 — The hook (SMS/call, 1 message)
> "Hey [Name] — I saw you're running [Emerge/NG/Frankies]. I built a free tool that handles scheduling + rain delays + invoicing for lawn crews. I'll set it up for you this week, free, no card. Worth 10 minutes?"

**Rule:** never lead with "I'm selling software." Lead with the free outcome. This is the §25 "value first" open.

### Step 2 — The call (the concierge sequence, §55)
Structure the 15-minute call exactly:
1. **Promise:** "By the end of this call you'll know if this can save you [5-10] hours a week."
2. **Story:** "I built this because I ran a lawn crew and kept losing jobs to missed texts. [One concrete pain story.]"
3. **Three concerns** (ask, don't assume):
   - "What happens now when it rains mid-week?" (→ rain-delay answer)
   - "How do you remember who's weekly vs biweekly?" (→ scheduling)
   - "What do you do for invoices?" (→ auto-invoice)
4. **Offer:** "I'll migrate your current clients in, set up your routes, and run your first week with you. Free for 30 days. If you don't love it, you keep everything we set up and cancel."
5. **Live FAQ** — answer from real DM buckets (from the concierge request data).

### Step 3 — The setup (you do it, not them)
Hormozi's first-10-customers rule: **unscalable personal effort.** You import their clients, set routes, configure the rain-delay banner, send the first texts. The operator's only job: approve.

### Step 4 — The win moment (capture proof — P1)
At the first delivered value:
- "How much time did that save you this week?" → **write down the answer, verbatim.**
- Screenshot the before/after: their old text-thread chaos vs. MowGo's schedule.
- If they praise it → ask for the testimonial then, not later. "Can I put that on our site? Real operators, real words."

### Step 5 — The conversion ask (at the win, not before)
- Day 30: "You're saving [X] hours a week. Want to keep it going at [$39/$79]? And if you pay for the year, it's [16%] off."
- **Annual-first** (the checkout test): present annual as the default option. "The annual plan is what most operators pick — one decision, done for the year."

## Fallback if a lead stalls

Hormozi's step-down script (from the library): offer quarterly billing instead of monthly, include a free first month with a testimonial commitment, or add a referral credit for bringing another lawn company. **Never discount the monthly price — change the billing structure or add value instead.**
