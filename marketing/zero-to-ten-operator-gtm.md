# MowGo Zero-to-10 Operator GTM

**Status:** Outreach-ready operating brief
**Audience:** Solo and 2-person lawn-care crews using texts, paper, Google Calendar, or basic/free software
**Stage:** 0–10 real paying operators

## The decision

MowGo is not launching as generic field-service software. For the first 10 operators, it is:

> **The simplest way for a solo or 2-person lawn crew to keep the week scheduled, handle rain changes, and get paid—without rebuilding the workflow every Sunday.**

The product can serve larger crews later. The first job is to prove this narrow operator will use MowGo during real work and pay for it.

## The offer

### Core offer: MowGo Concierge Pilot

> **Give us your current client list and schedule. We import it, set up your first operating week, and walk you through the real workflow. You use MowGo on actual jobs for 14 days.**

**Operational boundary:** The current product accepts a CSV/text client list and creates a concierge request; it does not yet guarantee that the first 30 days are automatically pre-scheduled by the backend. Treat “first 30 days pre-scheduled” as a founder-delivered service promise only after Aaron has reviewed the file and confirmed the schedule. Do not tell a prospect that setup is automatic.

Included:

- Client import plus first-week scheduling (the current admin workflow creates one-time jobs; recurring rules are configured afterward if needed)
- Founder-reviewed schedule setup for the first operating week; do not promise a 30-day setup until the operator's recurrence pattern has been confirmed
- One live setup call or guided setup exchange
- Route and navigation workflow
- Invoice workflow
- Rain-delay workflow remains owner-controlled: MowGo suggests options; the operator confirms any move
- Direct founder support during the pilot

### Risk reversal

> **If we do not get your first operating week set up, we keep working until it is done.**

Do not promise more jobs, revenue, hours saved, or automatic rescheduling until a real operator has produced and documented that result.

### Pricing test

- Free: first 5 clients, no card, self-serve
- Solo: $39/month or $390/year
- Crew: $79/month or $790/year
- Concierge is a setup and onboarding experience attached to paid plans, not a free data-entry service. A pilot operator must activate the 14-day Solo trial (or another eligible paid tier) before submitting the concierge request.

For the first 10 operators, the conversion question is simple:

> “After we set this up and you use it on real jobs, will you keep it at $39/month?”

A signup is not validation. Real usage plus payment is validation.

## Why this offer should win

- **Hormozi:** sells the operator's desired result and removes setup friction instead of leading with feature inventory.
- **Becker:** narrows the niche and sells the outcome before spending on traffic; no paid ads until organic conversion is proven.
- **Dan:** targets people already experiencing the problem, uses a pilot, and charges before calling the product validated.

## What the founder must do

1. Send the approved outreach below to 5 highly matched operators per weekday.
2. Ask about the current workflow before pitching.
3. Offer the concierge pilot only when the pain is present.
4. Help a qualified responder activate a Solo, Crew, or Premium trial/plan before submitting concierge; free-tier users cannot submit a concierge request.
5. Monitor the concierge queue, import clients, schedule the first week, review skipped rows, and mark the request done.
6. Record the exact current workflow and the first measurable win.
7. Ask for payment after the operator has used MowGo on real work.

## Qualification questions

Ask these in order:

1. “How are you scheduling recurring lawns today?”
2. “What happens when rain changes the day?”
3. “How do you know what is done and who still owes you?”
4. “What part of that process is most annoying or error-prone?”
5. “If someone imported your current clients and set up next week for you, would you try it on real jobs?”

Do not ask “Would you use an app?” That produces polite opinions, not evidence.

## 30-day acceptance criteria

MowGo earns the next product investment only if the outreach produces:

- 15 relevant conversations
- 3 active pilots
- 3 operators using MowGo during a real work week
- 2 documented measurable wins
- 1 paid Solo conversion
- 1 approved, genuine testimonial

If these do not happen, revisit the audience, pain, or offer before adding features.

## Proof rules

Never publish invented numbers, screenshots, customer names, or testimonials. Capture:

- Before workflow in the operator's words
- First win in the operator's words
- One real number: jobs moved, invoices paid, hours saved, or setup time
- Permission to use the quote and business context

## Product scope for this GTM phase

Keep the core path reliable:

1. Import or create clients
2. Schedule recurring jobs
3. View today's jobs and routes
4. Complete a job and create/send an invoice
5. See a rain suggestion and explicitly confirm any reschedule

Do not add AI, broad CRM functionality, or new automation as a substitute for proving this path.

## Sources used

- Dan Martell, “How To Validate Your Startup Ideas” (`3_A5JNLeHAQ`): paying competitor users, clickable prototype/pilot, pre-buying at a discount.
- Dan Martell, “How to Start a SaaS Business (Find the Pain)” (`0jjL3ZDpkxc`): inspect existing workflows/spreadsheets and find willingness to pay.
- Alex Becker, “How To Get 1,000 Paying SaaS Customer FAST From Scratch” (`EsgQ0bQzvZ0`): 0–10 customers requires direct acquisition and results, not scale infrastructure.
- MowGo proof-capture sequence: real-only proof at the operator's win moment.
- Current competitor research: Yardbook and Jobber create a real category and pricing floor; therefore MowGo must win on simplicity and assisted setup, not merely existence of scheduling features.

## Founder decision rule

At zero, the founder's job is not to “do marketing” broadly. It is to create 10 operator conversations, 3 real pilots, and the first paid proof that MowGo is better than the operator's current workaround.

Once that happens, the messaging, proof, and channel can scale.

Until then, outreach is the product discovery system.

# Copy-paste outreach

## Initial email — workflow-first

**Subject:** Quick question about your lawn schedule

Hi [Name]—I’m Aaron. I’m building MowGo for solo and small lawn crews.

Quick question: how are you handling recurring jobs, rain changes, and invoices today—Jobber/Yardbook, a calendar, texts, or something else?

I’m looking for a few Oklahoma operators willing to show me the real workflow. If it looks like a fit, I’ll import your clients and set up your first operating week so you can test it on actual jobs. No generic demo and no pressure.

What are you using today?

—Aaron

## Follow-up — pain-specific

**Subject:** Re: quick question about your lawn schedule

Hi [Name]—following up because I’m specifically trying to learn how small crews handle the week when weather changes.

What is the most annoying part for you right now?

1. Rebuilding the schedule after rain
2. Keeping routes and client notes together
3. Tracking completed work and unpaid invoices
4. Something else

If you want, I can show you the workflow using your first few real clients and set it up with you.

—Aaron

## Pilot invitation

Based on what you described, MowGo may fit.

I’ll personally:

- import your clients,
- set up your first operating week,
- show you the route, completion, invoice, and rain-delay workflows,
- and support you while you use it on real jobs for 14 days.

If it helps, Solo is $39/month after the pilot. If it does not fit, no hard feelings. Would you like to try it with your current schedule?

## Facebook/DM version

Hey [Name]—saw you run a small lawn crew in [City]. Quick question: are you scheduling recurring lawns through Jobber/Yardbook, a calendar, or mostly texts and memory?

I’m building MowGo specifically for solo/2-person crews. I’m taking a few operators through a hands-on pilot where I import the client list and set up the first operating week for them. I’m trying to learn the real workflow, not pitch a generic app.

What are you using today?

## Phone opener

“Hi, is this [Name]? I’m Aaron, a local developer building scheduling software for small lawn crews. I’m not calling to run you through a sales pitch. I have one quick question: how do you handle recurring jobs and rain changes today?”

If they engage:

“Would you be open to letting me see the workflow? If MowGo can help, I’ll set up the first week for you personally. If not, I’ll leave you alone.”

## Do not say

- “We will get you 30% more jobs.”
- “We have hundreds of customers.”
- “Nobody else does this.”
- “Automatic rain rescheduling” when the owner must confirm the change.
- Any customer number, testimonial, screenshot, or result that has not been verified.

- First real win

- Testimonial permission?
